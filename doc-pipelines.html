06 · Rules of thumb

Decisions that hold across every pipeline

  1. Use deterministic tools where the format is fixed and LLMs where it isn't. Prebuilt invoice models are cheaper and more consistent than a prompt; an LLM wins on long-tail layouts.
  2. Parse once, store a canonical form (Markdown or JSON with layout and page coordinates), and let every downstream pipeline read from it.
  3. Keep provenance: page, bounding box, source version. Citations, audits and human review all depend on it.
  4. Gate on confidence. High-confidence results flow straight through; low-confidence ones go to a human queue, and those corrections become your eval set.
  5. Make every stage idempotent and keyed by content hash, so reprocessing after a model or schema change is safe and cheap.
  6. Carry permissions and sensitivity labels from ingestion all the way to output. Redact before anything leaves the trust boundary.
  7. Measure per stage (parse accuracy, extraction F1, retrieval recall), not just end-to-end, or you won't know which stage broke.
  8. Budget by page. OCR and layout run roughly $1–15 per 1,000 pages, vision-LLM extraction often costs more, so route easy pages to cheap paths.
